`doctrine slice record-delta --commit <S>` records exactly `[S^, S]` — the commit's own patch — and that contract is what makes a *range* in the registry noticeable. `slice conformance` then computes the declared/undelivered/undeclared algebra over those recorded rows, so attribution follows the registry and never the git history underneath it.

Observed on SL-010's audit: PHASE-03's row ended at `108d1c2` rather than its own `cd69acd` — six commits, spanning another agent's "quotient" commit and two backlog commits. Nothing reddened; the undeclared cell simply read 14 paths instead of 4, and every note, sheet and handover still said `f26f923..cd69acd`. Re-recording `--start f26f923 --end cd69acd` returned 8 conformant / 0 undelivered / 4 undeclared, and `slice verify-vt` stayed green throughout — so VT and conformance can both be green over a mis-recorded span.

Audit rule that follows: before reading conformance's undeclared cell as scope creep or as a foreign commit, check each boundary row against the phase's own commit. `record-delta --start --end` is the unguarded escape hatch (UPSERT by phase), so the correction is one verb call; `--force` re-narrows a `--commit` record.
